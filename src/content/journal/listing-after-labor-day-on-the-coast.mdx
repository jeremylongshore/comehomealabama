Every August I get the same call from sellers. They meant to list in June, life happened, and
now they figure they've missed the season and ought to wait until spring. Six or seven months
of carrying a property they don't want, because of a rule of thumb they heard somewhere.

You didn't miss your window. On this coast the fall is a real selling season, and it has its
own rules. Here's what actually changes after Labor Day, which lands on Monday, September 7
this year.

## What the calendar does to your buyer pool

Tourist traffic drops off hard the week after Labor Day. Highway 59 loosens up, showings stop
competing with beach traffic, and the people still looking are the ones who are actually
buying. Summer tire-kickers go back to work. Fall shoppers have a reason and a timeline.

Your competition thins out too. Listings that sat all summer either come off the market or
adjust their price, and plenty of would-be sellers do exactly what you're thinking about
doing and wait for spring. Fewer comparable homes on the market in October is worth more to
you than more buyers in June.

Then November and December bring the snowbirds. That's a different buyer entirely: often
paying cash, already knows the area from years of renting here, and decisive because they're
only in town for a stretch. A house that shows well in December to somebody who has wanted
to come home to the coast for a decade is not a slow market.

## The insurance calendar is your real constraint

This is the part that costs fall sellers deals, and almost nobody brings it up until it's a
problem.

Hurricane season runs through November 30, and the statistical peak is right around
September 10, per NOAA. When a named storm is in the box, insurance carriers stop binding new
coverage. No binder means no loan, and no loan means no closing. That's not a maybe, and it
can freeze a deal for a week or more with your buyer's rate lock ticking.

You can't control the weather, but you can control how fast your side moves. Before you list,
put these in one folder:

- Your current wind and flood declarations pages, so a buyer's agent can get a real quote
  instead of a guess.
- Your claims history on the property.
- Roof age and any documentation you have. On this coast, roof age drives insurability more
  than almost anything else.
- Your wind mitigation inspection if you've had one done. If you haven't and your roof
  qualifies, getting one before you list can widen the pool of buyers who can actually insure
  the place.

A buyer who can get a quote in two days instead of two weeks is a buyer who closes before the
next storm name gets used.

## If you're selling a condo, front-load the documents

Condo contracts fall apart in the document review, not the showing. Have the last two
association budgets, the current reserve study, this year's insurance renewal summary, and a
written statement of any assessment that's been voted on or discussed ready on day one.

If your building has an assessment coming, say so up front and price for it. Buyers find out
anyway, and finding out in week three of escrow costs you the buyer plus the three weeks.
Naming it in week one costs you a negotiation you were going to have regardless.

## Pricing honestly in the shoulder season

The fall market punishes optimistic pricing faster than the summer market does, because there
are fewer buyers cycling through to correct your mistake. If you list high in June, twelve
people see it and you learn something. List high in October and you might get four showings
before Thanksgiving, and by then your listing looks tired.

Price it where the recent sales say it belongs, not where the summer listings are still
asking. Asking prices are what sellers hope. Sold prices are what buyers did. Only one of
those two is evidence.

One more thing worth confirming for whoever buys your place: Alabama's homestead exemption is
claimed with the county revenue commissioner, and the timing is tied to the fall assessment
period. Tell your buyer to call the Baldwin County Revenue Commissioner's office and confirm
their own deadline. It's a small thing that shows up on their tax bill for years.

## A short fall listing checklist

1. Insurance folder assembled before the first showing: declarations, claims history, roof
   age, wind mitigation.
2. Condo documents in hand, assessments disclosed up front.
3. Price set off closed sales, not off the listings that didn't sell all summer.
4. Photos reshot if yours are from a different season. Winter light on the water is a
   different picture than August, and buyers notice.
5. Contract timelines built with a week of slack. Appraisers and insurance carriers both slow
   down in the fall here.

You don't have to hold a property through the winter because June got away from you. You have
to be the listing that's ready when the crowd leaves and the serious buyers stay.
