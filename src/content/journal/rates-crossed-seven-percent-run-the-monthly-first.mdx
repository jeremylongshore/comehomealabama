The last two notes were about listings and prices. This month the number that moved
is the one most people check last: the mortgage rate. Here's what happened, and what I
think it means.

## What moved

Per Freddie Mac's weekly survey, the average 30-year fixed rate was 7.24 percent on
October 29. A year earlier it was 6.17 percent.

It got there fast. The rate bottomed near 6 percent in late February, crossed 7 percent
the last week of September, and sat at 7.40 percent for two weeks in October before
easing a little.

Asking prices haven't caught up to any of this yet. Realtor.com's county data for October
isn't out, but September had Baldwin County's median asking price flat from last year at
$499,000.

## What that does to the monthly

Take that $499,000 price with 20 percent down. At last fall's rate, principal and interest
come to about $2,437 a month. At last week's rate, about $2,721.

That's roughly $283 more every month, or about $3,400 a year, for the same house. Taxes,
wind and flood coverage, and any dues all come on top of that.

## If you're buying

If your pre-approval letter is from the spring, have your lender rerun it at today's rate.
The house you were approved for in April may not fit the same monthly now. Better to find
that out before you fall for one.

Then ask about the tools that soften it. A seller credit toward closing costs or a rate
buydown is a normal request in a market where homes are sitting close to 90 days. Ask your
lender to show you how long your lock lasts and what extending it costs.

Don't buy counting on a refinance. Rates might come down. They might not. Buy the
payment you can carry at the rate you actually get.

## If you're selling

Your buyer's budget shrank this fall even if your house didn't change. The same monthly
payment that covered a $399,200 loan last October covers about $357,600 now.

That doesn't mean you have to cut. It means you should price to what buyers can carry
today, and be ready for credit and buydown requests. Sometimes a credit does more for a
buyer than the same dollars off the price. Have your agent and their lender run both
before you answer.

These are national rate averages and countywide prices. Your quote will depend on your
credit, your down payment and your loan type, and a Fort Morgan condo doesn't price like a
new build off the Foley Beach Express. The averages tell you the weather, not your forecast.
