Two weeks out from Labor Day, about half the people who call me apologize for house hunting
right now. Highway 59 is backed up through Foley, every parking lot is full, and there's
somebody in every rental on the beach. They want to know if they should wait until things
calm down.

Wait if your timing needs it. Don't wait because it's crowded. Crowded is information you
cannot get in January, and nobody charges you for it.

## What a crowded tour shows you

**The commute, honestly.** Drive it at 5 on a Friday and again at 8 on a Tuesday morning.
Highway 59 in late August is the worst version of that drive, and the worst version is the
one worth knowing. If the Foley Beach Express toll is your workaround, price the tolls into
your monthly number instead of pretending you'll always take 59.

**Whether the parking actually works.** At a condo, ask how many spaces the building has
and how many units it has. August is the only month of the year when that ratio stops being
a number on paper. Same for the elevators, the pool deck and the beach walkover. Full
occupancy is the stress test.

**What the building sounds like at ten at night.** A floor with heavy rental turnover has a
different rhythm than a floor of owners. In February both floors are silent and you learn
nothing.

**Whether maintenance is keeping up.** Hallway carpet, elevator downtime, how fast the trash
rooms get cleared, whether anyone from the association is actually visible on site. A
building that looks tired in its busiest week is telling you something about its budget.

**The water.** Go stand at Alabama Point by Perdido Pass, then drive out Fort Morgan Road
and stand on that sand. Same county, different beach, different crowd, different price.

## What the crowd hides

Peak season flatters everything, so take two things off the table before you fall for it.

First, off-season is a genuinely different town. Hours shorten, some places close for a
stretch in the winter, and the street that felt alive in August is quiet in December. Plenty
of people move here specifically for that quiet. Just know which version you're buying, and
ask a local what January looks like on that block.

Second, if short-term rental income is part of your math, the August week is the top of the
range, not the average. Ask for twelve months of actual figures, not a summer projection.
An honest annual number is usually a lot less exciting and a lot more useful.

## The calendar that actually matters here

The market's rhythm on this coast runs on the weather and the calendar, not on national
headlines.

Right now through roughly September 10 is the statistical peak of the Atlantic hurricane
season, per NOAA. That matters to a buyer for one very practical reason: when a named storm
is in the box, insurance carriers stop binding new coverage. No binder, no closing. If you
go under contract in September, order your wind and flood quotes the same day, not the week
before closing.

Labor Day lands on September 7 this year. After it, tourist traffic drops off, showing
schedules loosen up, and the listings that sat through a whole summer start to see price
adjustments. That's the shoulder-season window, and it's usually the friendliest stretch of
the year for a buyer who did the looking in August.

Then the snowbirds arrive in November and December and the market gets quiet in a different
way, which is its own kind of negotiating room.

## If you're touring this week

1. Drive the commute twice, at the two worst times.
2. Count the parking spaces against the unit count, and say both numbers out loud.
3. On any condo, ask for the last two association budgets, the reserve study and this
   year's insurance renewal summary. Those three documents tell you more about your future
   monthly cost than the price does.
4. Walk the beach access at two in the afternoon, not at sunrise.
5. Ask somebody who lives there what the place is like in January.

## What it means for your offer

Nothing about a crowded tour should push you to move faster. It should let you move with
better information than the buyer who toured the same building on a quiet Tuesday in
February and thought the parking was fine.

If you go under contract this month or next, keep the insurance contingency, order the
quotes immediately, and give the appraisal a little extra runway. Closings slow down at the
end of summer here, and every year somebody finds that out the hard way.

You can come home to the coast without buying the postcard version of it. Tour it at its
loudest, run the numbers at your kitchen table, and decide when the crowd goes home.
