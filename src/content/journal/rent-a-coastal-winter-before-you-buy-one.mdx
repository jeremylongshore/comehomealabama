This time of year I start hearing from people who vacationed here every summer for fifteen years and are finally ready to move down. They know the coast. They've walked the beach at Gulf State Park, eaten at the same places, maybe rented the same condo in Orange Beach every June.

Here's the honest part. Most of them have never seen it in January.

That's not a reason to wait forever. It's a reason to rent one winter before you buy, because a month here between Thanksgiving and March answers questions a July week can't, and it costs a fraction of getting the purchase wrong.

## Why the summer version isn't the real version

A summer vacation shows you the coast at its busiest and its best-looking. Every restaurant is open late, the water is warm, and the traffic is so heavy you assume that's just how it is.

Winter flips almost all of that. The Gulf gets too cold for most people to swim. Nights in the 40s are normal, and a damp north wind off the bay feels colder than the number says. Some places on the beach road cut their hours or close for a few weeks. The roads open up, the parking lots empty out, and the towns settle into the rhythm of the people who actually live here plus the winter residents who come down from up north.

Some people find out they love that version even more. Some find out they only liked the vacation. Either answer is worth knowing before you sign a mortgage.

## How to set it up

Winter is the easiest season of the year to rent here. A lot of condo owners in Gulf Shores and Orange Beach offer monthly rates from roughly November through February because that's their slow season, and the monthly rate usually runs well under what the same unit gets by the week in summer.

A few things to ask before you book:

- **Is the rate monthly, and are taxes and cleaning included?** Lodging taxes and fees can add a real chunk to a short stay. Get the all-in number in writing.
- **What's included for utilities, internet and parking?** If you plan to work while you're here, test the internet speed the first day, not the last week.
- **Can you extend?** If the first month goes well, you may want a second one, and the good units get booked by returning winter residents.

You don't have to rent where you think you'll buy. In fact, it's often smarter to rent on the beach for the experience and spend the month driving inland to the places you can actually afford to own.

## What to test while you're here

The point of the month isn't to relax. Well, partly. But mostly it's homework, done in the season you'll be living in most of the year.

**Drive your real errands.** Grocery store, pharmacy, the hardware store, the gym or the golf course. Do them on a Tuesday morning and again on a Saturday afternoon. Then remember that summer adds traffic on top of what you're seeing.

**Drive to the doctor.** Find the urgent care and the hospital you'd actually use. For a lot of the south end of the county that's South Baldwin Regional Medical Center in Foley; on the Eastern Shore it's Thomas Hospital in Fairhope. Time the drive from each area you're considering.

**Cross the water both ways.** If you're looking at Orange Beach or Gulf Shores, the Intracoastal Waterway stands between the beach and almost everything inland. Learn the Foley Beach Express toll bridge, the Highway 59 bridge, and how each one behaves at different times of day. In July those bridges shape your whole day, and it's better to know that going in.

**Visit the towns you weren't planning to live in.** Spend a day in Foley, a day in Fairhope, a day in Spanish Fort, and a day across the line on Perdido Key. People are regularly surprised where they end up once they see what the same budget buys ten or fifteen minutes off the water.

**Go out after dark in January.** See what's open on a weeknight in February. If that matters to you, it matters a lot.

## What to price while you're here

This is the part that turns a nice month into a real decision.

Pick two or three actual addresses that look like what you'd buy, and get insurance quotes on them. Wind and flood coverage on the coast vary more by exact location than most people expect, and a quote on a real address beats any average you'll read online.

If you're comparing Alabama and Florida, look at the property tax and the insurance side by side. I walked through how both states handle taxes in the journal piece on retiring along the state line, and it's worth reading before you fall for one side.

And if a condo is on your list, ask for the building's dues history and any recent assessments. The winter is a good time to sit with those numbers, because you're not rushing back to the airport.

## The honest math on renting first

Buying the wrong house and selling it a year later costs you closing costs on the way in and selling costs on the way out, plus the moving truck twice. A winter rental is a fraction of that, and it gives you something no listing description can: what your Tuesday actually looks like here.
