Almost every seller I sit down with this time of year asks some version of the same question:
what should I fix before we list? Usually they've already got a list going, and usually the
expensive items are at the top. New countertops. A bathroom redo. Fresh flooring all the way
through.

Sometimes that's right. More often, the money that comes back to you at closing is the money
spent on boring things. Here's how I sort it.

## Start with the paperwork. It's free.

On this coast, buyers aren't only shopping the house. They're shopping the monthly cost of
owning it, and they can't get a real insurance quote without facts about your roof, your
windows and your flood zone. The sellers who have those facts ready get cleaner offers,
because the buyer isn't guessing.

Before you spend a dollar on repairs, gather:

- Your current homeowners and wind declarations pages, plus flood if you carry it
- The roof's age and the permit or invoice that proves it
- Any wind mitigation report, and a FORTIFIED certificate if you have one
- An elevation certificate, if one exists for the property
- Receipts for big systems: HVAC, water heater, roof, any impact windows or shutters
- For a condo, the current budget, the latest reserve study and anything about an
  assessment that's been voted or discussed

None of that costs money to pull together. It does take time, and it's better done now than
in the middle of an inspection period.

## Fix what the inspector will find

Every buyer's inspector writes a report, and every item on it becomes something to negotiate.
The small stuff is the stuff to get ahead of, because a long report full of small items makes
a buyer wonder what else got missed. On the coast, salt air and humidity put the same things
on the list over and over:

- **Corrosion.** Rusted exterior light fixtures, hose bibs, railings and hinges. The outdoor
  HVAC unit takes the salt hardest, so have it serviced and keep the receipt.
- **Soft wood.** Deck boards, stair treads, window sills and door jambs where water sits.
  Replace what's soft before someone pushes a screwdriver into it.
- **Water.** Stained ceilings, old leaks that were fixed but never repainted, slow drains,
  a dripping supply line. If a stain is from a leak that's been repaired, fix the stain and
  keep the repair invoice handy.
- **Doors and windows.** Sliders that drag on a gritty track, failed seals, locks that stick.
- **The obvious.** Missing outlet covers, a dead GFCI, smoke detectors past their date.

A handyman day and a service call often clear most of that list. Plenty of sellers get a
pre-listing inspection for the same reason, so nothing on the buyer's report surprises them.
If something big turns up, you get to decide how to handle it before you're on a deadline.

## Clean, paint, then stop

After repairs, the work that tends to pay is the work that makes the house photograph well
and feel cared for. A deep clean. Neutral paint where the walls are scuffed or loud. Pressure
washing the driveway, the siding and the pool deck. Trimmed landscaping. Fresh caulk around
tubs and sinks. Getting about half your stuff out of every closet and off every counter.

That's the list. It isn't glamorous, and that's the point.

## What usually doesn't come back

Big remodels right before listing are where sellers tend to lose money. The buyer wasn't there
when you picked the tile, and a lot of buyers plan to redo the kitchen anyway, to their own
taste. You can spend real money on a kitchen and get an offer from somebody who's mentally
gutting it on the showing.

Two other things that rarely pay off:

- **Over-improving for the street.** If your house is already one of the nicer ones nearby,
  the closed sales around you put a ceiling on the price. An upgrade that pushes past it mostly
  shows up as an expense.
- **Half-done projects.** A remodel you start and can't finish before photos is worse than one
  you never started. Finish it or leave it alone.

The exception is anything that changes the insurance math. A roof near the end of its life can
make a buyer's quote jump or make some carriers hesitate. That's a conversation worth having
with your agent and a roofer before you list, because it's tied to the monthly cost buyers
care about, not to taste.

## Tell the truth about what you know

Prep isn't hiding things. If you know about a problem, whether it's a past leak, a repair, or
water in the yard after a big storm, ask your agent how to disclose it properly. Disclosure
rules aren't the same in Alabama and Florida, and the right answer depends on the property.
Buyers on this coast ask about water and storms early, and an honest answer with a repair
receipt beats a surprise during the inspection every time.

## Your pre-listing order

1. Pull the insurance, roof, mitigation and system paperwork.
2. Get a pre-listing inspection, or at least walk the house with an inspector's eye.
3. Fix the small items: corrosion, soft wood, leaks, doors, outlets and detectors.
4. Service the HVAC and keep the receipt.
5. Clean, paint, pressure wash, declutter.
6. Talk through any big-ticket item with your agent before you spend on it.
