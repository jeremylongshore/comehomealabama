Late summer is when a lot of coastal policies renew, which means right about now the
letters are landing: your wind policy, your flood policy, maybe your condo association's
master policy summary. Most people see the auto-draft go through and never read page one.
This is your reminder to read page one.

## Three things to check before you auto-pay

**The premium, against last year's.** Not "does it feel high" — the actual number next to
the old number. Coastal premiums have been repricing for several years now, and a jump
isn't automatically wrong. But you can't shop what you haven't measured.

**The deductibles, especially the named-storm one.** Many coastal policies carry a
separate hurricane or named-storm deductible written as a percentage of your dwelling
coverage, not a flat dollar amount. On a bigger house, two percent is real money. Know
that number before a storm makes it relevant.

**What quietly changed.** Renewals sometimes arrive with a higher dwelling value, a new
exclusion, or a changed roof schedule. The one-page declarations summary tells you; the
auto-draft doesn't.

## When a jump means shop

If your renewal came in hard, an independent agent who writes multiple coastal carriers
can quote your exact house against the market in an afternoon. Sometimes there's a better
number. Sometimes the answer is that your carrier is still the good deal, and now you
know. Either way you decided something instead of drifting.

## When it changes the bigger math

For some owners, this year's letter is the third jump in a row, and it quietly rewrites
the monthly cost of the place. If that's you, it's worth an honest hour with the whole
picture: what you're carrying, what your equity looks like, and what the same monthly
number buys a mile or two inland. Not because you have to move. Because deciding with the
numbers in front of you beats deciding by auto-draft.
