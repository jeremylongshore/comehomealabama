Most people meet Gulf Shores on vacation. They drive south on Highway 59 until the road runs
out at the beach, check into a condo, and spend a week between the sand and the same three
restaurants. That version of Gulf Shores is real. It's just a small piece of the town you'd be
buying into, and the rest of it is where most of the ownership math happens.

## What Gulf Shores actually is

Gulf Shores is a city in Baldwin County, and Highway 59, the Gulf Shores Parkway, is its spine.
The parkway runs south through the city, crosses the Intracoastal Waterway and ends at Beach
Boulevard. From there, West Beach runs one way, Gulf State Park takes up the other, and Fort
Morgan Road heads west down the peninsula.

Gulf State Park matters more to owners than most people realize. It's more than 6,000 acres of
public land on the east side of town, with beach, lakes and trails. That's ground nobody is
going to build a tower on, which shapes how that end of town feels year after year.

## The canal is the line that matters

The Intracoastal Waterway splits Gulf Shores in two, and the two halves are different
purchases.

South of the canal is the beach side: Gulf-front and near-beach condos, beach houses, the
lagoon side of West Beach, and most of the vacation rentals. You're closest to the water, and
you're also closest to the costs that come with it. Wind and flood coverage, association dues
on the condo side, and elevation all weigh heaviest here.

North of the canal, the city keeps going up the Highway 59 corridor toward Foley. That's where
you'll find more single-family neighborhoods and newer construction, with the beach a drive
over the bridge instead of a walk across the street. Insurers often rate wind partly by
distance from the Gulf, so the same budget can buy a different monthly payment depending on
which side of the water the house sits. Get quotes on the actual address. Don't guess.

## Know your bridges

Every trip between those two halves crosses the Intracoastal. The Gulf Shores Parkway bridge
carries Highway 59 over the canal. East of it, toward The Wharf in Orange Beach, the crossing
changed this spring. Per ALDOT's May 13 announcement, a new Intracoastal Waterway bridge now
carries southbound traffic, and the former toll bridge carries northbound traffic.

More lanes help. They don't change the basic fact that on a June Saturday, everyone who wants
to reach the beach is using the same few crossings you are. Drive the route from the house
you're considering to the grocery store and to work twice: once in peak season, once on a
quiet weekday in November. If a hurricane evacuation ever comes, that bridge is also your
way out.

## Rental income has rules

If rent is part of your plan, read the city's rules before you read the rental projections.
Per the City of Gulf Shores rental license page, property rented in the city's corporate
limits or police jurisdiction needs a license, and short-term rentals carry:

1. A $45 short-term rental fee on top of a business license fee based on gross receipts.
2. A rental safety inspection every three years.
3. A designated local emergency contact the owner has to maintain.
4. Lodging tax the owner collects and remits.
5. A Planning and Zoning review for zoning compliance.

Licenses expire December 31 and become delinquent after January 31, which matters if you're
closing on a rental in the next couple of months. Out on Fort Morgan Road, some property sits
in unincorporated Baldwin County, so confirm which jurisdiction the parcel is actually in
before you assume which rules apply. And association documents can narrow the answer further
with minimum stays or outright rental limits.

## What to go in eyes-open about

Flood zones vary block by block here, especially near Little Lagoon and the canal. Pull the
parcel on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center, and see the journal's post on flood-zone math
before you price a lot or a raised house.

If school assignment is part of your decision, know that Gulf Shores City Schools has run as
its own system since 2019, separate from Baldwin County's. Confirm the specific address with
the system directly rather than assuming by ZIP code.

And remember that this is a visitor town. Summer brings traffic, full restaurants and busy
beach accesses. Winter is quieter, and plenty of owners will tell you that's their favorite
season here. Know which one you're buying for.

## Who it fits

Gulf Shores works best for buyers who want the beach as part of the ordinary week, not just a
destination, and who are clear about which side of the canal fits their budget and their
daily drive. It also suits owners who want public land next door and the option of rental
income, as long as the parcel and the documents support it.
