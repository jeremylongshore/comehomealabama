Every few weeks someone calls me about a piece of land they found online. The price looks
right, the photos show pretty pines, and the listing says "bring your builder." Here's what
I tell them: the sticker price on raw land is the beginning of the math, not the end of it.

My family builds and clears land here, so I've watched the cheap parcel become the expensive
one more times than I can count. This is the checklist I'd walk through with you before we
ever talk about writing an offer.

## 1. Can it pass a perc test?

If the parcel isn't on sewer, you need a septic system, and a septic system needs soil that
drains. That's what a percolation test measures. In parts of Baldwin County the soil is
sandy and drains beautifully. In other parts you hit clay or a high water table, and suddenly
you're pricing an engineered system that can run several times the cost of a conventional one.

Ask whether the seller has a recent perc test or a soil evaluation on file. If not, make your
offer contingent on one. That single contingency has saved my clients real money.

## 2. Where's the power, and who serves it?

A power pole at the road is one thing. A homesite 600 feet back in the trees is another,
because you'll pay to bring service in. Utility providers vary across the county, and so
do their policies on how much line they'll run before the meter starts costing you per foot.
Get the actual number from the actual provider for the actual homesite. Estimates from the
listing agent don't count.

Same conversation for water: county water at the road, a well, or a long tap run. Each one
is a different line on your budget.

## 3. What will clearing actually cost?

"Wooded and private" reads lovely in a listing. It also means mobilizing equipment, taking
down trees, grinding stumps, and hauling debris. Clearing costs depend on how thick the
growth is, how wet the ground is, and how far the debris has to travel. A building pad plus
a driveway through soft, heavily treed ground is a very different job than knocking down
brush on high sandy ground.

Before you commit, get someone who clears land for a living to walk it and give you a real
number. I can usually make that call the same week.

## 4. Check the flood zone and the drainage, not just the elevation

South Baldwin is flat and close to the water table. Two parcels a quarter mile apart can sit
in different flood zones, and that changes your insurance, your foundation design, and
sometimes whether a lender will touch it. Pull the FEMA map, then stand on the land after a
hard rain if you can. Water tells the truth.

## 5. Know how land loans work before you shop

Most banks treat raw land as a different animal from a house. Expect a bigger down payment,
a shorter term, and a higher rate than a mortgage. Local banks and farm credit lenders are
often the most comfortable with dirt. If you plan to build soon, ask about a
construction-to-permanent loan instead, because one closing beats two.

None of this is meant to scare you off land. It's some of the best money my clients have
put to work on the coast. It just rewards people who do the homework before the offer,
not after.
