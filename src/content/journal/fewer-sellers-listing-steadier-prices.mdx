Last month the story was softer prices. This month it's quieter sellers. Here are the
numbers, and what I think they mean.

## What moved

Per realtor.com's county research data through September, 546 new listings came on the
market in Baldwin County. That is about 24 percent fewer than last September. Active
listings came in at 2,838, roughly 9 percent below a year ago.

Prices held. The median asking price was $499,000, essentially flat from last year. The
number I flagged in July was price per square foot, which was running about 6 percent
under the year before. By September that gap had narrowed to about 1 percent, at $324 a
foot.

Median time on market was 87 days. That's about five days quicker than last September.

Across the line in Escambia County, Florida, the same data put the median at $336,725,
with a median 72 days on market.

## If you're buying

The houses that are listed aren't getting cheaper the way they were this summer, and
fewer new ones are coming behind them. If you've been waiting for prices to keep sliding,
September didn't cooperate.

That doesn't mean you lose your leverage. About 17.6 percent of Baldwin listings had a
price cut in September, so an offer under ask is still a normal conversation. And 87 days
is still a slow market. You have time to order the wind and flood quotes and read the
condo documents before you commit. Use it.

## If you're selling

Fewer new listings means less fresh competition this fall, which helps. But don't read a
flat median as a rising one. Buyers have plenty of homes that have been sitting a while
to compare you against, and they know which ones have been cut.

If you're thinking about listing before the snowbirds arrive, the sellers who stayed off
the market this month left you a little more room. Price it to what's closing now and
show up ready, and you'll stand out in a thinner field.

Same caution as every month: these are countywide numbers, and a Fort Morgan condo,
a Fairhope cottage, and a new build off the Foley Beach Express don't move together. The
county tells you the weather, not your forecast.
