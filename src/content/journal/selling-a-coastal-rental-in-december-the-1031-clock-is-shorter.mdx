This time of year I hear the same plan from a lot of rental owners. Sell the Orange Beach condo before the year turns over, roll the money into something with less upkeep, and use a 1031 exchange so the gain doesn't get taxed on the way out.

It's a good plan. It also comes with a clock, and the clock runs differently when you close in November or December. Most people find out about that part in March.

## What a 1031 actually does

Section 1031 of the tax code lets you sell real property you hold for business or investment and put off the tax on the gain, as long as you buy like-kind real property that you'll also hold for business or investment. Since 2018 it only applies to real property. A condo you rent out and a rental house in Foley count as like-kind to each other. So does a lot you're holding as an investment.

Two plain limits. A house you live in or use as a vacation place doesn't qualify. And the tax is deferred, not forgiven. It follows you into the next property.

## The two deadlines

The statute sets both, and neither one bends for holidays.

- **45 days** after you close on the property you're selling, you have to identify what you're buying. In writing, signed by you, and delivered to someone in the exchange who isn't you or your own agent.
- **180 days** after that same closing, you have to own the replacement property. Or by the due date of your tax return for that year, including extensions, if that comes first.

That last clause is the one that matters in December.

## A December closing, worked

Say your condo closes on **December 1, 2026**.

- Day 45 is **January 15, 2027**. Your identification window runs straight through Christmas and New Year's, which is not when sellers are returning calls.
- Day 180 would be **May 30, 2027**.
- But for most individual filers, the 2026 return is due **April 15, 2027**. That's 135 days after closing, not 180.

If you file your return on time in April, your exchange has to be finished by then. If you file for the automatic extension instead, the due date moves to October, and you get the full 180 days back. A CPA can tell you in two minutes whether that's the right call for you. The point is that somebody has to make it on purpose before mid-April.

The rough cutoff for 2026: a sale that closes on or after **October 18** has a 180th day that falls past April 15, 2027. Anything closing from now through the end of the year is inside that window.

## Your identification options

You can't just name everything on the market. The regulations give you two main ways to do it:

1. **Up to three properties,** at any price.
2. **Any number of properties,** as long as their combined value isn't more than 200 percent of what you sold.

Most people I work with take the first option and pick three properties they'd actually close on, ranked. A backup matters here. Coastal deals slip for reasons nobody controls, including insurance binders, surveys and appraisals.

## Don't touch the money

In a typical delayed exchange, the sale proceeds go to a qualified intermediary, not to you. The rules are strict about this. If you can receive, borrow against or otherwise get the benefit of that cash before the exchange is finished, you can lose the exchange. So the intermediary has to be signed up **before** your closing, and the settlement statement should show the proceeds going to them. Arranging it after the funds land in your account is too late.

Any cash you do take out, or debt you don't replace, is generally taxable even when the rest of the exchange holds.

## If you used the condo yourself

This is the coastal question. A lot of owners rent through a program most of the year and keep a few weeks for themselves. That doesn't automatically disqualify the unit, but it muddies it.

The IRS gave a safe harbor in Revenue Procedure 2008-16. A dwelling unit counts as held for investment if you owned it at least 24 months before the exchange, and in each of the two 12-month periods before the sale:

- you rented it to others at a fair rental for **14 days or more**, and
- your own use didn't go over **14 days or 10 percent of the days it was rented**, whichever is greater.

Rent it 200 nights and you can use it up to 20. Pull your rental program's owner statements for both years before you list. A similar test applies to the property you buy.

## One Alabama detail for out-of-state owners

If you live outside Alabama, Alabama law can require the buyer to withhold Alabama income tax from your proceeds at closing unless an exemption applies. The Alabama Department of Revenue lists a like-kind exchange among the exemptions when the gain isn't recognized for Alabama tax. Raise it with your closing agent early, so the paperwork is ready when you get to the table.

## Before you list

- Talk to a CPA about whether the gain is worth deferring and whether to extend.
- Sign the qualified intermediary agreement before closing.
- Start shopping for the replacement now, not after closing.
- Pull two years of rental and personal-use records.
- Put the 45th day and the true last day on your calendar the day you go under contract.

None of this is tax or legal advice, and your CPA and intermediary get the final word on your exchange. The math only works if the dates do.
