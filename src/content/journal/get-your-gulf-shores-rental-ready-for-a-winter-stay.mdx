A winter booking deserves its own preparation. Someone unpacking for several weeks will use the kitchen, laundry, storage, and internet differently from someone spending a weekend at the beach. A clean unit is the starting point. Clear expectations and working equipment finish the job.

September is a useful time to walk your Gulf Shores rental before winter arrivals. Open the closets, run the appliances, and read the arrival message as though you've never seen the property. The coffee maker shouldn't be the most reliable member of the management team.

Here's the order I'd use to get the property and the handoff ready.

## Start with the calendar and the building

Ask your property manager and, for a condo, the association about planned work during the proposed stay. Ask specifically about balcony access, elevator service, painting, pool maintenance, and any work inside the unit. Get the current schedule in writing, including what is still uncertain.

Compare that schedule with the amenities described in your rental advertisement. If access may be interrupted, have the manager explain it before someone books. Don't promise an uninterrupted stay based on last winter's calendar.

Also ask the manager to verify that the proposed stay length fits the property's rental rules and operating requirements. A monthly booking needs its own review; changing the dates on a summer confirmation isn't the whole job. Resolve open questions before accepting the reservation.

## Walk the unit beyond the cleaning checklist

Run a normal cycle on the dishwasher and washing machine. Check the refrigerator shelves, cabinet hinges, lamps, window coverings, and door locks. Sit in the chairs and look under the sinks. Write down each repair, who will handle it, and when you'll verify it is finished.

Have the appropriate service provider check anything that needs technical attention. A note saying the heating system is scheduled for service is different from a completed service record. Keep the record with the property file.

Include fire safety in that walk. The U.S. Fire Administration's [vacation rental checklist](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/hotel-fires/) calls for working smoke alarms in sleeping rooms, outside separate sleeping areas, and on every level. It also tells guests to identify two ways out of each room and check that exterior doors and windows open. Use that checklist with your manager, and ask the local fire department about requirements for your property.

Don't let stored owner belongings block an exit or crowd the space advertised for guest use. Photograph the finished rooms so the manager has a dated reference for the handoff.

## Put utility and service expectations in plain language

Read the proposed rental paperwork alongside the advertisement. Can a guest tell which utilities and services are included? If there is a usage allowance or separate charge, ask the manager to explain how it is measured, documented, and billed before the booking is accepted.

Check the internet instructions from inside the unit. Connect a device using the guest instructions, rather than a saved password on your own phone. Confirm the provider's support contact and decide who the guest should call first if service stops.

Make cleaning expectations just as clear. State whether any cleaning during the stay is included, how additional service is arranged, and what departure requires. Have the manager put agreed terms in the proper paperwork. An arrival binder should explain those terms, not introduce new charges.

## Give the address its own instructions

Local details matter more than a generic welcome sheet. Gulf Shores' [residential garbage schedule](https://gulfshoresal.gov/314/Trash-Recycling) distinguishes areas north of 2nd Avenue from the Beach Rental District south of and including it. The city also lists separate container timing for Beach Boulevard properties. Confirm the instructions for your address instead of copying a neighboring rental's sheet. For a condo, get the building's disposal instructions from management.

That city page also lists a 2026 special recycling window of September 26 through October 5 for items including electronics, fluorescent bulbs, and paint. If your fall preparation involves replacing those items, check the current drop-off details before loading the car.

For arrival, spell out the building name, unit number, parking arrangement, and door-entry steps. Test the instructions with someone who hasn't used them before. Keep access codes in the private guest message, not in the public advertisement.

## Leave one clear path for help

Give the guest a primary management contact and an after-hours contact. Explain that emergencies go to 911, and include the property's full address where it is easy to find. Ask the manager how guests should report a leak, failed appliance, or lost access and how you'll receive those reports.

Before releasing the unit, review the repair list one last time. Every unfinished item needs a decision: finish it, explain its effect honestly, or adjust the booking plan. Keep copies of the final instructions and the agreed rental terms together.
