Most people who call me about Spanish Fort aren't really asking about Spanish Fort. They're
asking about a job in Mobile, a spouse who works at the hospital across the bay, or a
contract at Brookley, and they want to know if they can live on the Eastern Shore and still
make that drive work. Spanish Fort is usually the first honest answer. It's also the town
where one road project is about to matter more than anything else on the list.

## What Spanish Fort actually is

Spanish Fort is the northern anchor of the Eastern Shore. It sits on high ground above the
Mobile-Tensaw Delta, right where I-10 comes off the Bayway and lands in Baldwin County.
That position explains almost everything about it. It's the closest Baldwin County town to
downtown Mobile, and it grew up as the place people live when they work on one side of the
bay and want a home on the other.

It is not a beach town and doesn't pretend to be. What it has instead is the delta. Historic
Blakeley State Park is just up the road, Meaher State Park sits right on the Causeway, and
the Five Rivers Delta Resource Center is the easiest way to get a kayak onto water most
visitors never see. The retail side of town clusters around Eastern Shore Centre and
Spanish Fort Town Center, so groceries and errands don't require a bay crossing.

## The bridge news, in plain numbers

On September 18, ALDOT announced that financing is complete for the Mobile River Bridge
and Bayway Project, and that full construction begins on October 1. Phase One is a $3.2
billion build: a new cable-stayed bridge over the Mobile River and a rebuilt Bayway with
three travel lanes in each direction. ALDOT expects the new bridge to open to traffic in
about five years, which puts it around late 2031.

Here's the part that belongs in a buyer's spreadsheet. The new route will be tolled, and
tolling only begins when the new bridge and Phase One improvements open. Per ALDOT,
passenger vehicles will pay $3 per trip with an ALGO Pass, and frequent users will have a
$60 monthly option for unlimited crossings. The Wallace Tunnel, the Bankhead Tunnel, the
Africatown Bridge and the Causeway all stay toll-free.

So the honest version is this. If you cross every workday and want the new bridge, budget
$60 a month starting around 2031, the same way you'd budget for dues or flood insurance.
If you'd rather take a free route, you can, and Spanish Fort sits right at the Causeway.
Nobody should buy or skip a house over a toll that doesn't exist yet. But you should know
the number, and you should know it's five years out, not next spring.

## The next five years are the real question

The toll gets the headlines. The construction is what you'll actually live with. A project
this size runs for years along the exact corridor Spanish Fort commuters use, and I'm not
going to guess at lane closures or detour schedules that ALDOT hasn't published. What I'd
tell a buyer is simple: if your daily drive crosses the bay, drive it at your real commute
hour before you write an offer, and check the project's traffic updates for that week.
Then decide whether the free routes are your plan B or your plan A.

## Taxes change by where you shop

One small thing locals know and newcomers don't. Spanish Fort's own tax page lists three
different total sales tax rates inside the city: 8.5% in most of Spanish Fort, 9.5% at
Eastern Shore Centre, and 10.95% at Spanish Fort Town Center. Those totals include the 4%
state and 3% county pieces. It won't make or break a move, but it's the kind of detail that
shows up on every receipt, and it's worth knowing before you're surprised by one.

## What to go in eyes-open about

Honest numbers cut both ways. The same high ground that gives Spanish Fort its views means
hills, slopes and ravines, so drainage and grade deserve a real look on any lot or house.
Walk the yard after a hard rain if you can. Being up on the bluff doesn't take you out of
the coastal insurance conversation, either. The Alabama Insurance Underwriting
Association's eligible area covers Baldwin County south of the 31st parallel, and Spanish
Fort is inside it. Get an actual insurance quote on the specific address before you
commit, same as you would at the beach.

And if the reason you're moving here is sand, Spanish Fort is the wrong end of the county.
The beach is a real drive from here, not a quick one. Some people are fine with a weekend
trip. Some would regret it every Saturday in July. Know which one you are.

## A word on resale

Spanish Fort is mostly a primary-home town. Demand here tends to follow jobs on both sides
of the bay rather than vacation cycles, which usually gives it a steadier floor than
second-home markets that pause when buyers get nervous. That's a principle, not a promise.
Every street and price point trades on its own. But the bridge project is a long-term bet
on this exact corridor, and that's worth noticing when you think about who your eventual
buyer will be.
